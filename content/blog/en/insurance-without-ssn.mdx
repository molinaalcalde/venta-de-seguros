In the United States, not having a Social Security Number does not mean going without insurance. Millions of people access auto, life, health, and commercial insurance using alternative documents like an ITIN, consular ID, or foreign passport.

Yet misinformation on this topic remains widespread. According to the Census Bureau, more than 4.4 million people in the U.S. hold an active ITIN (IRS, 2023). Many of them don't realize they already qualify for multiple types of insurance.

This guide explains exactly what you need, what insurers accept, and how the process works by insurance type and state.

## What Is an ITIN and How Does It Work for Insurance?

The ITIN (Individual Taxpayer Identification Number) is a 9-digit tax identification number issued by the IRS. It is not a Social Security Number, does not authorize employment, and does not grant immigration status. Its original purpose is to enable compliance with federal tax obligations.

What most people don't know: insurers use it as a valid identification to evaluate your risk profile and issue policies. When an insurer requests a "tax ID," the ITIN fulfills that requirement.

### How to Get an ITIN

If you don't have an ITIN yet, you can apply through the IRS by completing Form W-7 along with your federal tax return. Processing takes between 7 and 11 weeks (IRS, 2026). No specific immigration status is required to obtain one.

## Documents Insurers Accept as Alternatives to SSN

Not all insurers request the same documents. Here are the most common alternatives:

**ITIN**: Accepted by most major insurers for auto, life, and commercial insurance. This is the most widely accepted SSN alternative.

**Consular ID (Matrícula Consular)**: Issued by Mexican and other Latin American consulates. Accepted by some auto insurers, especially in California, Texas, and Illinois.

**Foreign Passport**: Accepted as photo identification. Usually combined with proof of U.S. residence.

**State Driver's License**: In the 19+ states that issue licenses regardless of immigration status, this license serves as primary identification for auto insurance.

**Alien Registration Number (A-Number)**: For permanent residents and individuals with pending immigration cases. Widely accepted.

## Auto Insurance Without an SSN: State by State

Auto insurance is arguably the most accessible insurance type without an SSN because it's legally required in nearly every state. Insurers have a financial incentive to offer accessible options.

### Insurers That Accept ITIN for Auto Insurance

Several of the largest insurers in the country accept ITIN or don't require an SSN to issue an auto policy. Among them: GEICO, Progressive, State Farm, Allstate, and The General. Subject to terms, conditions, and availability by state.

Carriers that don't require an SSN will typically use your address and name to calculate an insurance score instead of your traditional credit history (The Zebra, 2026).

### States With Driver's Licenses Regardless of Immigration Status

Currently, 19 states and the District of Columbia allow driver's license issuance regardless of immigration status. The most significant include:

- **California**: AB-60 law. Over 1.2 million licenses issued under this law (California DMV, 2024).
- **Illinois**: TVDL (Temporary Visitor Driver's License).
- **New York**: Green Light Law, effective since 2019.
- **New Jersey**: Law since 2021. Accepts foreign passport and proof of residence.
- **Texas**: Does not have a specific non-SSN license, but some insurers accept ITIN directly.

Having a state driver's license opens the door to auto insurance because it eliminates the primary identification barrier.

### How Much Does Auto Insurance Cost With an ITIN?

The cost varies by state, age, driving history, and vehicle. However, using an ITIN instead of an SSN generally does not directly affect the premium price. What can affect pricing is the lack of U.S. credit history, since in most states insurers use credit as a pricing factor (Consumer Federation of America, 2024). Subject to current state regulations.

## Life Insurance Without an SSN

Life insurance is accessible for people with an ITIN. Insurers primarily evaluate your health and ability to pay, not your immigration status.

### Insurers That Accept ITIN for Life Insurance

Insurers that accept ITIN as identification for life policies include Prudential, New York Life, MassMutual (through Haven Life), Mutual of Omaha, Nationwide, and Guardian through its Global Citizens program. Subject to terms, conditions, and underwriting approval.

Prudential is considered the most immigrant-accessible among major carriers, with published guidelines on accepted visa types and documentation (InsuranceProviders.com, 2026).

### Does It Cost More With an ITIN?

In some cases, the cost may be 10% to 20% higher than for someone with an SSN, depending on the insurer and medical profile (ricoenusa.com, 2026). However, many insurers do not apply a price differential for ITIN use.

### Options Without ITIN or SSN

If you have neither an SSN nor an ITIN, guaranteed issue policies and final expense policies exist. These do not require a medical exam or extensive tax documentation. Coverage is more limited and the cost per dollar of benefit is higher, but it's a real option. Subject to provider terms and conditions.

### International Beneficiaries

Many life insurance policies allow you to designate beneficiaries who reside outside the United States. This is especially relevant for families with loved ones in their home country. Payment is made in U.S. dollars. Consult with your agent about each insurer's specific procedures.

## Health Insurance Without an SSN

Health insurance options depend significantly on your state of residence and immigration situation.

### Marketplace (ACA/Obamacare)

Federal Marketplace plans require an SSN or qualifying immigration status to access federal subsidies. The ITIN does not directly qualify for ACA subsidies (Healthcare.gov, 2026).

### Real Options Available

- **Private health plans**: Many insurers offer individual plans that accept ITIN as identification. No federal subsidy, but real coverage.
- **Community Health Centers (FQHCs)**: Over 1,400 community health centers in the U.S. serve anyone regardless of ability to pay or status (HRSA, 2026).
- **State programs**: Some states like California (Medi-Cal), Illinois, and New York have expanded coverage to residents regardless of immigration status.
- **CHIP**: The Children's Health Insurance Program covers eligible children in many states.

### Medical Emergencies

The EMTALA (Emergency Medical Treatment and Labor Act) requires any hospital with an emergency room to treat anyone with a medical emergency, regardless of ability to pay or status. This does not eliminate the subsequent bill, but it guarantees immediate care.

## Business and Pet Insurance Without an SSN

For business insurance (commercial, professional liability) and pet insurance, identification requirements are generally more flexible than for auto or health.

In most cases, a valid photo ID and a payment method are sufficient. For business insurance, you'll need business documentation (EIN if you have one, or the owner's ITIN).

## What You Should Know About Privacy

This is one of the most legitimate and frequent concerns. It's important to understand how it works:

**Applying for insurance does not generate immigration reports.** Insurers are private companies regulated by state departments of insurance. Their interest is evaluating financial and health risk to calculate your premium. They have no obligation or incentive to report information to immigration agencies.

**Your information is protected by law.** Insurers operate under strict federal and state data privacy laws, including the Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act, which regulates how financial institutions handle personal information.

**An independent agent works for you.** Unlike a captive agent who represents a single company, an independent agent represents you. Your information is shared only with the insurers you authorize for quotes.

## The Real Cost of Going Uninsured

The financial consequences of living without insurance are concrete:

- **Auto**: An uninsured accident in New Jersey can result in fines of $300 to $1,000, license and registration suspension, and personal liability for all damages caused. The average accident's repairs and medical expenses exceed $20,000 (III, 2024).
- **Health**: The average hospital stay in the U.S. costs $13,262 (KFF, 2024). Without insurance, that bill is 100% yours.
- **Life**: If the family's primary earner passes away without life insurance, the family loses its income source immediately. For families sending remittances, the impact multiplies across two countries.

Insurance is not a luxury. It's the tool that turns an emergency into a manageable setback instead of a financial catastrophe.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I get auto insurance without a driver's license?

In some states, it's possible to be a policyholder without having a license (for example, if someone else drives your vehicle). Requirements vary by state and insurer. Consult with an independent agent to evaluate your case.

### Do insurers verify my immigration status?

No. Insurers verify your identity, address, and depending on the insurance type, your medical or driving history. They do not verify or report immigration status.

### Can I use a consular ID instead of an ITIN?

For auto insurance, yes in some states and with some insurers. For life insurance, an ITIN or passport is generally required as tax identification. Requirements vary.

### What happens if my ITIN is expired?

ITINs that haven't been used on tax returns for 3 consecutive years expire automatically. You can renew it by submitting Form W-7 to the IRS. In the meantime, some insurers accept the expired ITIN along with proof that renewal is in process.

### How long does it take to get a quote?

With an independent agent, you can get quotes from multiple insurers in a single consultation. The typical process takes between 15 and 30 minutes.

### Do I need to speak English to get insurance?

No. Many insurers and independent agents offer service entirely in Spanish. A bilingual agent can guide you through the entire process in your language.

### Can I have insurance in one state and live in another?

Your insurance must be from the state where you reside and where your vehicle is registered (for auto). If you move states, you need to update your policy. An independent agent licensed in multiple states can facilitate this transition.
