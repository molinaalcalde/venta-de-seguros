The Hispanic community in the United States has the lowest life insurance penetration rate of any demographic group. According to LIMRA, only 51% of Hispanics have any type of life coverage, compared to 54% of the general population (LIMRA, 2024). The gap widens when looking at coverage amounts: Hispanic families with insurance are significantly underinsured.

This matters because the economic structure of many immigrant families relies on a single income supporting expenses in two countries. If that income disappears, the impact is immediate and compounds across borders.

This guide explains how life insurance works, what options exist for non-citizens, and how to evaluate which type of coverage makes sense for your situation.

## How Life Insurance Works

Life insurance is a contract between you and an insurer. You pay a monthly or annual premium, and in return, the insurer commits to paying a specific amount (death benefit) to the people you designate as beneficiaries if you pass away during the policy period.

That money can cover:
- Funeral expenses (the average cost of a funeral in the U.S. is $7,848 according to the NFDA, 2023)
- Outstanding debts (mortgage, auto loan, credit cards)
- Family living expenses during the stabilization period
- Remittances that family in the home country will stop receiving
- Children's education

The death benefit is generally paid free of federal income tax (IRS Publication 525).

## Types of Life Insurance

### Term Life Insurance

The most straightforward and affordable option. It covers you for a defined period: 10, 20, or 30 years. If you pass away during that period, your family receives the full benefit. If the term ends and you're alive, coverage ends with no cash value.

**Who it's for:** Families with growing children, people with a mortgage or other large debts, or anyone who needs maximum coverage at the lowest cost during their years of greatest financial responsibility.

**Reference cost range:** A healthy 30-year-old can get a $500,000, 20-year term policy starting at approximately $20-30/month. At 40, the range increases to $35-55/month for the same coverage. Prices vary significantly by health, gender, and insurer. Subject to underwriting approval.

### Whole Life Insurance

Covers your entire life with no expiration date. In addition to the death benefit, it accumulates a cash value that grows over time and that you can access during your lifetime (through policy loans or partial withdrawals).

**Who it's for:** People seeking lifelong protection combined with a savings component. Can also function as an estate planning tool.

**Cost consideration:** Whole life premiums are 5 to 15 times higher than term life for the same coverage amount. This doesn't make it better or worse, just different in purpose. Subject to terms and conditions.

### Universal Life Insurance

Similar to whole life but with more flexibility. You can adjust your premiums and coverage amount within certain limits throughout the policy's life. The cash value is invested and grows based on the policy type (fixed rate, indexed, or variable).

**Who it's for:** People with variable income who need payment flexibility, or those who want an investment component within their insurance.

### Living Benefits: Accessing Money While Alive

Many modern policies include living benefits. This means that if you're diagnosed with a terminal illness, a chronic condition, or a critical illness like cancer or a severe heart attack, you can access a portion of the death benefit while still alive.

This is not an automatic benefit in all policies. Ask specifically whether the policy you're evaluating includes accelerated benefit riders for terminal, chronic, or critical illness. Subject to the terms and conditions of the contracted policy.

## Life Insurance Without Citizenship: What You Need to Know

Most insurers in the United States do not require citizenship to issue a life policy. The most common requirements are:

1. **Reside in the United States** (verifiable address)
2. **Valid identification**: ITIN, foreign passport, or Alien Registration Number
3. **Medical questionnaire** or medical exam (depending on coverage amount)
4. **U.S. bank account** for premium payments (premiums must be paid in U.S. dollars from an American account)

### Insurers That Accept ITIN

Not all insurers have the same level of accessibility. These are recognized for their openness to clients with ITIN:

- **Prudential**: Considered the most accessible among major carriers for immigrants. Publishes clear guidelines on accepted documents and visa types.
- **New York Life**: Approves green card holders and many visa categories. Agents who speak multiple languages.
- **Haven Life** (backed by MassMutual): Fully digital term policies. Approves many H-1B and L-1 visa applicants.
- **Mutual of Omaha**: Accepts ITIN for life and supplemental policies.
- **Guardian (Global Citizens program)**: Specifically designed for international clients.

Subject to terms, conditions, and underwriting approval depending on the provider and state.

### The Underwriting Process With ITIN

The evaluation process is similar to that of any applicant:

1. **Application**: Personal data, medical history, risk activities
2. **Medical exam** (if applicable): Generally includes blood pressure, blood and urine analysis. For lower amounts, many insurers offer "no-exam" policies
3. **Identity verification**: This is where you present your ITIN or alternative document
4. **Evaluation**: The insurer classifies your risk (preferred, standard, rated)
5. **Offer**: You receive the offer with your premium price

Applying for life insurance does not generate any immigration report. Insurers evaluate health and financial risk, not immigration status.

## International Beneficiaries: How It Works

This is one of the most relevant aspects for immigrant families. If your beneficiaries live in another country:

- **It is possible** to designate international beneficiaries on most policies
- **Payment is made in U.S. dollars**, generally via international wire transfer or check
- **Taxes**: The death benefit does not pay federal income tax in the U.S. (IRC Section 101). However, the beneficiary may have tax obligations in their country of residence. Consult a tax professional
- **Documentation**: The beneficiary will need a death certificate, completed claim form, and copy of their identification

Important note: designating specific beneficiaries (full name, date of birth, relationship) prevents the benefit from going through probate, which can be costly and slow.

## Remittances and Life Insurance

In 2023, remittances from the United States to Latin America and the Caribbean reached $155 billion (World Bank, 2024). Millions of families in the region depend on these transfers for basic expenses, education, and healthcare.

If the person sending remittances passes away without life insurance, those families lose their source of support immediately. There's no transition period. There's no automatic safety net.

A life policy with international beneficiaries is, in practical terms, the only way to ensure that flow of support continues long enough for the family to reorganize.

## How Much Coverage Do You Need?

The general rule financial planners use is 10 to 12 times your annual income. For someone earning $50,000 per year, that would mean $500,000 to $600,000 in coverage.

However, if you regularly send remittances, experts suggest adding $200,000 to $300,000 to cover that flow (InsuranceProviders.com, 2026).

Factors to consider:
- Current debts (mortgage, auto, credit cards)
- Number of dependents
- Monthly remittance amount multiplied by years of dependency
- Funeral expenses and repatriation if applicable
- Children's future education

An independent agent can help you calculate the right amount without pushing you toward a more expensive policy than you need. Subject to underwriting approval.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I get life insurance if I don't speak English?

Yes. Many insurers and agents offer full service in Spanish. Policy documents may be in English, but a bilingual agent can explain every detail before you sign.

### What happens to my policy if I return to my home country?

It depends on the insurer. Some policies remain active as long as you continue paying premiums, even if you change residence. Others require U.S. residency. Check specific conditions before purchasing.

### Can I have more than one life insurance policy?

Yes. There's no legal limit on the number of policies. Many people combine a large term policy for their years of greatest responsibility with a smaller permanent policy for lifelong coverage.

### How long does approval take?

For term policies with a medical exam: 2 to 6 weeks. For simplified no-exam policies: a few days to 2 weeks. Guaranteed issue policies: immediate approval in many cases.

### What is a final expense policy?

It's a permanent life policy with coverage typically between $5,000 and $25,000, designed to cover funeral expenses and minor debts. Generally has simpler underwriting requirements and is available to older individuals or those with health conditions. Subject to terms and conditions.

### Can the insurer deny a claim if I don't have citizenship?

No. If the policy is active and premiums are current, the insurer is contractually obligated to pay the benefit. Your immigration status at the time of death is not a factor in claim payment.
